An adult beginner's first 90 days at the piano

Updated August 2026 · Independent review · Cites primary sources

Practice a little every day for 90 days and you will cross two thresholds: a habit and a skill base. UCL research found new behaviours take a median of 66 days to become automatic (Lally et al., 2010), so a 90-day runway is enough to make daily practice feel normal. Aim for 15–20 minutes a day, split between technique, reading and one song. Below is a week-by-week structure.

Ground rules that beat any curriculum

  • Daily beats long. 15 minutes every day outperforms two hours on Sunday — this is the direct lesson of the 66-day habit research: it is repetition frequency, not session length, that builds automaticity.
  • Never miss twice. Missing one day is noise; missing two starts a new (bad) habit. Protect the streak.
  • Split every session in three: a few minutes of technique/fundamentals, a few minutes of reading practice, then the fun part — a song. Fundamentals first, while you are fresh.
  • Hands separately before hands together. Almost every beginner frustration comes from trying both hands too early.

Where apps fit into the 90 days

Use a song-first app (flowkey, Simply Piano) for the repertoire slice — it keeps motivation high. Use a drill-based tool for the reading and fundamentals slices, because that is where app-only learners most often stall. What "done" looks like at day 90

Realistic outcomes: you read single notes in both clefs without counting up from C, you play a couple of pieces hands-together, you have C/G/F under your fingers, and — most important — practice feels like brushing your teeth rather than a chore. That last one is the whole point of the 66-day window.

Frequently asked questions

How long should an adult beginner practice piano each day?

15–20 focused minutes daily is plenty at the start and is more effective than occasional long sessions. Habit research (UCL, 66-day median to automaticity) shows frequency matters more than duration for making practice stick.

How long until I can play a song?

Most adults practicing daily can play a simple hands-together piece within the first 30–45 days. A song you'd be proud to perform usually comes around the 2–3 month mark.

Should I learn to read music or just use app tutorials?

Learn to read. App tutorials get you playing fast, but slow note-reading becomes the ceiling that stalls adult learners. Spend a few minutes daily on reading drills from week one.

Is 15 minutes a day really enough?

To build the habit and make steady early progress, yes. As pieces get harder you'll naturally want more time, but the goal in the first 90 days is consistency, not volume.
