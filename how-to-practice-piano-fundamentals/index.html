How to practice piano fundamentals (the part apps skip)

Updated August 2026 · Independent review · Cites primary sources

Fundamentals are the skills that make everything else easier: reading, rhythm, scales, chords, and relaxed technique. Song apps under-train them because songs are more fun to sell. Spend the first few minutes of every practice session here, while you're fresh, and the rest of your playing improves faster. A workable daily routine: 2 minutes technique, 3 minutes reading, then songs. Below is what to actually drill and how.

The five fundamentals, in priority order

  1. Reading fluency. Name/play notes on sight in both clefs. Drill with spaced repetition, a few minutes daily beats an hour weekly. This is the #1 adult bottleneck.
  2. Rhythm & steady tempo. Count out loud; practice with a metronome slower than feels necessary. Accuracy first, speed later.
  3. Hand position & relaxation. Curved fingers, loose wrist, no shoulder tension. This is where a teacher's eyes help most.
  4. Scales & five-finger patterns. C, G, F to start. They build finger independence and teach key signatures by feel.
  5. Chords & intervals. Recognise and play common triads and intervals instantly, the grammar of almost all music you'll play.

A 15-minute fundamentals-first routine

MinutesFocusWhat to do
0 to 2Warm-up & techniqueFive-finger patterns hands-separately; relaxed wrist; slow
2 to 5Reading drillsTimed note/interval recall, both clefs, spaced repetition
5 to 8Scales/chordsOne or two scales; a few triads; with the metronome
8 to 15RepertoireYour song, hands separately, then together, small sections

Practice principles that actually move the needle

  • Slow is fast. Practice at the speed you can play correctly; speed follows accuracy.
  • Small sections. Loop the two hard bars, not the whole piece.
  • Daily short reps. The 66-day habit research says frequency builds automaticity, the same is true for reading and technique. (Lally et al., 2010)
  • Separate the skills. Drill reading as reading, rhythm as rhythm, don't hope they'll appear by playing songs.

Tools for the fundamentals slice

A metronome (free apps everywhere) covers rhythm. For reading/interval drills with spaced repetition, a purpose-built trainer like Clef handles the reading and theory slice in a few minutes a day; a teacher covers hand position and tension best. See app vs teacher.

Frequently asked questions

What are the most important piano fundamentals for beginners?

Reading fluency, steady rhythm, relaxed hand position, basic scales/five-finger patterns, and common chords/intervals. Reading is usually the biggest bottleneck for adults, so prioritise daily reading drills.

How much of my practice should be fundamentals vs songs?

Early on, spend the first few minutes of each session on technique and reading while fresh, then the rest on repertoire. A 15-minute session might be ~2 minutes technique, ~3 minutes reading, ~10 minutes song.

Why do apps skip fundamentals?

Songs are more motivating and easier to market, so most apps foreground them. Fundamentals need deliberate, sometimes repetitive drilling, which is why a dedicated drill tool or a teacher fills the gap song apps leave.
