Digital piano buying guide for beginners

Updated August 2026 · Independent review · Cites primary sources

For a first digital piano, get 88 weighted keys with touch sensitivity from a reputable brand, the Roland FP-10 and Yamaha P-145 are the two standard entry recommendations. Then budget a little extra for a stand, a sustain pedal, a bench and headphones. Skip anything with fewer than 88 keys or non-weighted keys if you're serious about piano technique. Below is exactly what matters and what doesn't.

The four specs that matter

  1. 88 weighted (hammer-action) keys. Non-negotiable for real piano technique.
  2. Touch/velocity sensitivity. Harder = louder. Essential for dynamics.
  3. Polyphony of 64+. How many notes sound at once. 64 is fine to start; 96+ is better for pedal-heavy pieces.
  4. A trusted brand action. Roland, Yamaha, Kawai, Casio all make solid graded-hammer beginner boards. This is where cheap no-name keyboards fail.

Digital vs acoustic for a beginner

For most adult beginners, a weighted digital piano wins: silent practice with headphones, no tuning, portability, MIDI for apps, and a lower entry price. An acoustic upright has a feel and resonance many players eventually prefer, but it is a bigger, louder, higher-maintenance commitment. Start digital; upgrade later if the hobby sticks.

Frequently asked questions

What digital piano should a beginner buy in 2026?

An 88-key weighted digital piano with touch sensitivity from a trusted brand. The Roland FP-10 and Yamaha P-145 are the two standard entry recommendations; Casio and Kawai also make good beginner boards. Add a stand, pedal, bench and headphones.

How much should I spend on a first digital piano?

Budget for the instrument plus a stand, pedal, bench and headphones. Prices shift by retailer, so compare current dealer pricing rather than trusting an old figure. The entry weighted pianos above are designed to be the affordable serious choice.

Is a digital piano good enough, or do I need an acoustic?

A weighted digital piano is more than good enough to learn on and offers silent practice, no tuning and app connectivity. Many players use one for years. Consider an acoustic later if you develop a preference for its feel.

Do I need a MIDI keyboard to use learning apps?

No. Apps like Simply Piano and Skoove can listen through your device mic. A MIDI connection (USB or Bluetooth on the FP-10/P-145) makes app feedback more accurate but isn't required to begin.
