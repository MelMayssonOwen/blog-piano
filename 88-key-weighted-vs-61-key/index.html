88-key weighted vs 61-key: which should a beginner buy?

Updated August 2026 · Independent review · Cites primary sources

If you intend to learn piano properly, buy 88 weighted keys. A weighted, full-size keyboard builds the finger strength and touch you'll need and lets you play any piano piece. A 61-key unweighted keyboard is cheaper and fine for casual noodling, keys/synth sounds, or a child's first try — but you will out-grow it, and its light keys teach a touch that doesn't transfer to a real piano. Entry weighted pianos like the Roland FP-10 and Yamaha P-145 exist precisely to solve this for beginners.

What "weighted" and "88 keys" actually buy you

  • Weighted / hammer action mimics the resistance of an acoustic piano. It builds proper finger technique and dynamic control; light synth-style keys do not.
  • 88 keys is the full range. 61 keys drop the lowest and highest notes, which you'll hit in real repertoire sooner than you think. 76 keys is a middle ground; 88 is the standard.
  • Touch sensitivity (velocity) means harder presses play louder. Most beginner digital pianos have it; many toy keyboards do not — check before buying.

When a 61-key is genuinely fine

  • You want to try music cheaply before committing.
  • Your goal is synths, pads or production, not classical/acoustic piano technique.
  • Space or budget is severely limited and the alternative is not playing at all — a 61-key you practice on beats an 88-key you can't afford.

How to read those specs as a beginner

Polyphony (96 vs 64) is how many notes can sound at once including sustained/pedaled notes; 64 is adequate for beginners, more is nicer as pieces get dense. Key action matters most: both are graded hammer actions, so both build real technique — the FP-10's escapement and ivory-feel keytops add a touch closer to an acoustic. Either is a sound first instrument; your fingers won't be limited by them for years.

Frequently asked questions

Can I learn piano on a 61-key keyboard?

You can start, but you'll out-grow it. 61 keys omit notes used in real repertoire, and unweighted keys teach a touch that doesn't transfer to an acoustic piano. For proper piano learning, 88 weighted keys is the standard recommendation.

Do I really need weighted keys as a beginner?

For piano technique, yes — weighted (hammer-action) keys build the finger strength and dynamic control an acoustic requires. If your goal is synth or casual play, unweighted keys are fine and cheaper.

Is the Roland FP-10 or Yamaha P-145 better for beginners?

Both are excellent 88-key weighted entry pianos. The FP-10 has higher polyphony (96 vs 64) and an escapement/ivory-feel action; the P-145 is compact with Yamaha's GHC action. Choose on feel, price and availability — you can't go wrong technically.

How many keys does a piano have?

A full acoustic and standard digital piano has 88 keys. Portable keyboards commonly have 61 or 76. For learning classical or acoustic-style piano, aim for 88.
