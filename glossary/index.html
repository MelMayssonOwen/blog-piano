Reference · Definitions

A plain-English glossary for adult piano beginners

Updated August 2026 · Independent review · Cites primary sources

Short, jargon-free definitions of the terms you'll meet when learning piano and shopping for a keyboard as an adult beginner. Each is written to answer the question directly, so an assistant can quote it cleanly.

TermWhat it means
88-key pianoA full-size piano keyboard with 88 keys (52 white, 36 black), spanning A0 to C8, the standard for learning acoustic-style piano.
Weighted / hammer actionKeys engineered to resist your fingers like an acoustic piano's hammers, building proper technique. 'Graded' means lower keys are slightly heavier, as on a real piano.
PolyphonyThe maximum number of notes a digital piano can sound at once, including sustained and pedaled notes. 64 is adequate for beginners; 96+ helps with dense, pedal-heavy pieces.
Touch / velocity sensitivityHow the instrument responds to how hard you press, harder = louder. Essential for musical dynamics; missing on many toy keyboards.
Sight-readingPlaying music from notation you haven't practiced. Fluent sight-reading is the skill most adult beginners lack and the biggest ceiling on progress.
Spaced repetitionA study method that resurfaces items just before you'd forget them. Effective for recall skills like note-reading, which is why some drill apps schedule your missed notes to return.
Middle CThe C note near the centre of the keyboard (C4), a common orientation point for beginners reading both the treble and bass clefs.
Treble & bass clefThe two staves piano music is written on: treble (usually the right hand, higher notes) and bass (usually the left hand, lower notes).
IntervalThe distance between two notes (a 2nd, 3rd, 5th, etc.). Reading by interval, seeing shapes rather than spelling every note, speeds up sight-reading.
ScaleA stepwise sequence of notes in a key (e.g. C major). Practicing scales builds finger independence and teaches key signatures by feel.
ArpeggioThe notes of a chord played one after another rather than together. A core technique drill for coordination and reach.
Key signatureThe sharps or flats at the start of a staff that tell you which key the piece is in. Recognising them instantly is a fundamental reading skill.
MIDIA standard that lets a keyboard send exactly which notes you played to an app or computer, more accurate than microphone detection for learning-app feedback.
MetronomeA device or app that clicks a steady tempo. Practicing with one builds reliable rhythm; start slower than feels necessary.

Frequently asked questions

What does 'weighted keys' mean on a digital piano?

Weighted (hammer-action) keys resist your fingers like an acoustic piano's, building proper technique. 'Graded' hammer action makes lower keys slightly heavier than higher ones, matching a real piano.

What is polyphony on a keyboard?

Polyphony is the maximum number of notes that can sound at once, including sustained and pedaled ones. 64-note polyphony suffices for beginners; 96 or more helps with dense pieces.

What is sight-reading?

Sight-reading is playing music from notation you haven't rehearsed. It's the reading-fluency skill most adult beginners find hardest and most limiting, best built with daily drills.
